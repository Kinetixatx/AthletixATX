What is body composition?

Body composition describes the proportions of fat, lean tissue, and bone in your body. Two people can weigh the same but have very different body composition. Following these measurements over time can reveal changes that a bathroom scale misses.

DEXA Scans

DEXA estimates total and regional body fat, lean soft tissue, and bone mineral content. Consistent scan conditions help make repeat comparisons more useful.

Visceral Fat

Visceral fat sits deep in the abdomen around internal organs. Higher amounts are associated with greater cardiometabolic risk, even when body weight appears normal.

Lean Mass & Strength

Lean tissue supports physical function and performance. Resistance training and adequate nutrition help maintain and build muscle. DEXA lean mass is not identical to muscle mass.

Bone Health

Bone density matters for long-term resilience. Your results should be interpreted in context with age, medical history, and appropriate clinical guidance.

A CLOSER LOOK

Visceral fat deserves special attention.

Unlike the fat just beneath your skin, visceral fat collects inside the abdominal cavity. Excess visceral fat is linked with insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and other metabolic health concerns. It is possible to have a normal body weight and still have elevated visceral fat.

Why it matters—and what you can do

A visceral fat estimate is a risk indicator, not a diagnosis. Regular exercise, strength training, nutrition habits that support a healthy body composition, sufficient sleep, and appropriate medical follow-up can help improve overall metabolic health. Your individual results deserve context, not alarm.
